# Design

## Context

See proposal.md for why. As of `main` 8dbf4150 (0.20.0-preview.13; every line below re-read there, none moved since the review): `Domain\RemovalDecision.cs:8-14` is one record of six members built by three private factories, `WorktreeRemover.cs:18-23` switches on its `Outcome` enum and reads `Forced`, `Branch` and `PruneRegistration` from the same value, and `WorktreesView.cs:75-77` compares `Outcome` with `RemovalOutcome.Refused` before reading `Reason` and `CanBeForced`. `Domain\WorktreeObservation.cs:13-20` is seven members; `WorktreeInventory` builds it at lines 43, 124, 175, 191 and 198, `WorktreeInspector.HealthAsync:30-31` twice, and `WorktreeHealth.From:9-17` relies on its four `RegisteredWithGit: true` arms coming before the five that read the pointer. `GitPointer.TargetExists` is built only by `WorktreeInventory.TargetOf:235`, called only from `OrphanRows`, where every observation has `UnderClaudeWorktrees: true`; `WorktreeHealthShould:107-113` pins it with `UnderClaudeWorktrees: false`, which nothing builds, while `WorktreeInventoryLeftoversShould.KeepAnOrphanWhosePointerNamesARegistrationThatStillExistsAnOrphan` pins the case that is built. `Domain` and `Ports` declare 43 abstract records (31 and 12, `Ports\LastRun`, `Ports\PackageNotes` and the reshaped `UpdateCheckRecord.Checked` from PR #180 among them), and `Session` and `RemoteRepository` are the two whose cases are top-level (`BackgroundSession.cs`, `InteractiveSession.cs`, `GitHubRepository.cs`, `AzureDevOpsRepository.cs`); no type outside Core derives from any of them; `Ports\StartResult(string Message)` is the precedent for a positional base with nested cases, and no hierarchy in Core declares a private constructor. `DeleteSessionOutcome.cs:9` holds `TabClosed` as an init property that `DeleteSessionHandler.cs:22` sets with `with`. The two session case names appear in 56 files (160 occurrences) across src, test and tools, and the repository cases in 14.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Every member of a `RemovalDecision` or `WorktreeObservation` case is meaningful for that case, so no factory fills a placeholder and no consumer reads a member the case does not own.
- Every outcome in `RemovalDecisionShould`, `WorktreeHealthShould`, `WorktreesViewShould`, the two handler tests, `WorktreeInventoryLeftoversShould`, `WorktreeInspectorShould` and the real-git `WorktreeInventoryShould` is unchanged: the facts are rewritten to the new shapes, never to new expectations.
- The nesting convention in `CLAUDE.md` is held by a reflection fact, not by review.

**Non-Goals:**
- Any change to which health a folder gets, which removals are refused, or what is shown or deleted.
- Reshaping `WorktreeRemovalFacts`, `WorktreeRow` or `RemoveWorktreeOutcome`.
- `Application`'s open hierarchies (`Outcome`, `DiagnosticEvent`, `StatusMessage`), whose cases are top-level by design and outside the new fact.

## Decisions

**`RemovalDecision` is `Refused | RemoveWorktree | DeleteFolder`, and its placeholders go.** `Refused(RemovalReason Reason, bool CanBeForced)`, `RemoveWorktree(bool Forced, BranchAction Branch)`, `DeleteFolder(bool Forced, bool Prune)`; `For` keeps its arms and the three private factories become the three constructors. `WorktreeRemover.CarryOutAsync` switches on the case (three arms, a discard throwing `UnreachableException` as the two-case rule extends to three), passing `RemoveWorktree` and `DeleteFolder` to the two private methods so each reads only its own members; `WorktreesView.Remove` becomes `decision is RemovalDecision.Refused refused`. `RemovalOutcome.cs` is deleted. With no case to fill, `BranchAction.None`, `RemovalReason.None` and `RemovalReason.LeftoverFolder` have no producer (`RemovalDecision.cs:29,33,35` are the only ones today, and `CarryOutAsync` surfaces a reason only from `Refused`), so they go with `Wording.cs:110,116` and `WordingShould:500,542` (`SayNothingForAPlainRemoval`, `CallALeftoverFolderByThatName`); `WorktreeRemover.cs:43`'s branch arm becomes `(_, Head.Detached or Head.Unknown)`, and CS8509 points at any switch that still names them. Alternative: keep the placeholders as "nothing to explain" cases. Rejected: a case nobody constructs is the placeholder the review objected to, moved one type over.

**`WorktreeObservation` is `Registered | Unregistered`, and `From` switches on the case first.** `Registered(bool FolderExists, WorktreeLock Lock, bool Prunable)` and `Unregistered(GitPointer GitFile, bool UnderClaudeWorktrees, bool EmptyLeftover)`; `From` is two private methods, one per case, so the four registered arms and the five unregistered arms can no longer be interleaved. Honestly stated: order still carries meaning inside a case, as a priority the facts pin, `FolderGone` before `GitFileGone` (`BeBrokenWithTheFolderGoneWhenAPrunableFolderIsGone`) and the pointer arms before `UnderClaudeWorktrees` (`ReportAnOrphanWhosePointerNamesAGoneRegistrationAsBrokenWithoutCountingThePointer`). The inventory's five sites each build the case they observed: line 43 `Unregistered(NoGitFile, false, EmptyLeftover: true)`, line 124 `Registered(folderExists, entry.Lock, entry.IsPrunable)`, line 175 `Unregistered(pointer, UnderClaudeWorktrees: true, false)`, lines 191 and 198 `Unregistered` with `RegisteredElsewhere(repository)` and `TargetGone`. Alternative: keep one record and add a reflection fact that `From` never mixes the groups. Rejected: a fact about arm order is weaker than a shape that makes the order impossible.

**`WorktreeInspector.HealthAsync` answers `FolderGone` directly for an unlisted folder that is gone.** Today's line 30 builds `RegisteredWithGit: !folderExists` so a folder git does not list and that is gone lands on the `FolderGone` arm; `Unregistered` has no `FolderExists`, so the inspector answers `new WorktreeHealth(Broken, new HealthReason.FolderGone())` for unlisted-and-gone and `From(new Unregistered(new NoGitFile(), false, false))` for unlisted-and-present, with its summary saying why. `ReportTheFolderGoneWhenGitNoLongerListsItAndTheFolderIsGone` and `NeverReportTheFolderGoneWhenGitDoesNotListItButTheFolderExists` pin both. Alternative: build a `Registered(FolderExists: false, Unlocked, Prunable: false)` for something git does not list. Rejected: that is a false observation made to steer the switch, the pattern this change removes.

**`GitPointer.TargetExists` stays, and its fact moves to the case the code builds.** `WorktreeHealthShould.BeOrphanWhenTheRegistrationExistsButNoOwningRepositoryCanBeNamed` becomes `Unregistered(new TargetExists(), UnderClaudeWorktrees: true, false)` expecting Orphan with `UnderClaudeWorktrees`, and `TargetExists`'s summary says it is the honest reading of an orphan's `.git` file and never changes the health under `.claude\worktrees`. The review's preferred fix, `TargetOf` answering `RegisteredElsewhere(RepositoryOf(gitDir))` so such a folder becomes Elsewhere and is never deleted, is not taken here, for three reasons the owner may weigh differently: it changes the requirement at `openspec\specs\worktree-management\spec.md:20` ("a folder under a repository's .claude\worktrees that the listing omits is an orphan"), so it needs a change with a delta spec and cannot sit under `skip_specs`; `WorktreeInventoryLeftoversShould.KeepAnOrphanWhosePointerNamesARegistrationThatStillExistsAnOrphan` and the archived `null-free-observations` design record the current behaviour as deliberate ("an orphan never names an owner"); and the realistic producer under `.claude\worktrees` is git listing the same folder under another spelling (a junction, as `WorktreeInspector.HealthAsync`'s summary notes), where the owner is the scanned repository itself and `RepositoryOutsideRoots`'s words ("add that root in settings") would be false. The security rule in `.claude\rules\security.md` scopes the Elsewhere promise to folders directly under a root or the worktree root, which this change keeps. Alternative: drop `TargetExists` and let `TargetOf` answer `NoGitFile`. Rejected: the folder has a `.git` file, so that reading would be untrue, and `TargetOf` would need a misnamed branch.

**`DeleteSessionOutcome(bool TabClosed)` is positional on the base and passed through by every case.** `public abstract record DeleteSessionOutcome(bool TabClosed) : Outcome` with `Deleted(string Name, bool TabClosed) : DeleteSessionOutcome(TabClosed)` and the same on the other five, so `WordingOfOutcomes.Gone` keeps reading the base, the theory in `WordingOfOutcomesShould` sees the flag positionally, and `DeleteSessionHandler.DeleteAsync` threads `tabClosed` into `RemoveWorktreeAsync` instead of `with`. Alternative: an abstract `TabClosed` property overridden by a positional member on each case. Rejected: twice the lines for the same shape, and `StartResult` already shows the pass-through form.

**`Session` and `RemoteRepository` nest their cases; `RemoteRepository` gets a private constructor, `Session` keeps its positional base.** `Session.Background` and `Session.Interactive` move into `Session.cs` with their summaries, and `RemoteRepository.GitHub` and `RemoteRepository.AzureDevOps` into `RemoteRepository.cs`, the four top-level files deleted (`OneTypePerFileShould` holds). `RemoteRepository` is parameterless, so `private RemoteRepository() { }` costs one line and closes it to other assemblies; its `GeneratedRegex` methods `GitHub()` and `AzureDevOps()` are renamed `GitHubUrl()` and `AzureDevOpsUrl()` because a nested type and a method cannot share a name. `Session`'s base has five positional members and a primary constructor is always public (`CLAUDE.md`), so a private one would mean hand-written properties for a guarantee the new fact already gives Vantage's assemblies; `StartResult` is the precedent for leaving it positional. The rename is mechanical and compiler-driven (`BackgroundSession` to `Session.Background`, `InteractiveSession` to `Session.Interactive`; no clash with `Palette.Background` or `TabEscapes.Background`, which are always qualified), lands as one commit with no logic edit, and the `not BackgroundSession` arm at `SessionsView.cs:68` and the discard at `SessionRowEnricher.cs:63` stay as they are. Alternative: record the two as the deliberate exception in `CLAUDE.md` and an allow-list. Rejected: 160 occurrences in a compiler-driven rename is an afternoon, and an exception list for two types is a standing reminder that the rule has holes.

**A reflection fact with no allow-list.** `Architecture\ClosedHierarchiesShould.NestEveryCaseInsideItsBase`: for every abstract record declared in the `Source191.Vantage.Domain` and `Source191.Vantage.Ports` namespaces, every type in the Core assembly deriving from it has that record as its `DeclaringType`. Scoped to Domain and Ports because `Application`'s `Outcome` and `DiagnosticEvent` have one top-level case per file by design (PR #179 added more `DiagnosticEvent` cases that way). Over `main` today it names exactly the four files above and nothing else: the hierarchies PRs #178 and #180 added all nest their cases. Alternative: extend `OneTypePerFileShould`'s Roslyn walk. Rejected: that fact is about files, this one is about types, and reflection is how the other `Architecture` facts work.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [`fix-removal-paths` edits `WorktreeRemover` and `DeleteSessionHandler` at the same time] → it lands first; this change rebases, and its edits to those two files are shape changes a rebase resolves by hand in minutes. It also removes `BranchToDealWith` and adds two `RemovalReason` cases, so the branch arm at `WorktreeRemover.cs:43` and `HaveWordsForEveryRemovalReason` are re-read after the rebase.
- [A 56-file rename hides a logic change] → the rename is its own commit with no other edit; the diff is reviewed for names only, and every test keeps its expectations.
- [`HealthAsync` builds one `WorktreeHealth` outside `From`] → the two inspector facts pin both answers, and the summary says the disc, not git, decided.
- [Dropping `RemovalReason.None` and `LeftoverFolder` removes words] → neither was ever shown: `RemoveWorktreeOutcome.Refused` and `StatusMessage.NotRemoved` only ever carry a reason from `Refused`, and `HaveWordsForEveryRemovalReason` enumerates the nested types that remain.
- [The owner wants the Elsewhere behaviour for orphans after all] → it is a separate change with a delta spec against `worktree-management`; this change leaves `TargetOf` and `RepositoryOf` where that change would need them.

## Migration Plan

Nothing to migrate: no state file, key, setting or command line changes. Rollback is reverting the pull request.
